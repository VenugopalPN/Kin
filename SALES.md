# Showing Kin — four minutes

Everything lives in one browser. Gifts, websites, and campaigns stay on this computer — two laptops in the same meeting will not share them. If anything looks off partway through, the **Reset demo** link in the bottom-right corner clears it back to the start.

Kin opens with one example already filled in, **Wattle Primary**, running two fundraisers at once — library books and new sports gear — each with its own gifts already in, so every door opens onto something finished, even before you touch anything. Anyone can start a campaign the same way.

The pages are stacked bands, not a form beside a preview. A full-width black-and-white photo comes first. An orange ribbon cuts across the bottom of that photo. A white band, then a dark band, follow. The four doors are one tall photo tile and three shorter tiles — white, orange, and an outline — on a black page.

## The four doors (`/`)

Say: *"Giving and getting go together. Four people touch this: the platform, the campaign, a promoter, and the person giving."* The doors read **Platform**, **Campaign**, **Promoter**, and **Family**. Tap a door.

## 1. Platform — create a website

`/platform`

The bar reads **Create a website**, with the line "You are creating a website." The photo fills the top and shows the name as you type. The fields are **Name** and **Place**, and **Create the website** is the orange ribbon. **Fill demo values** sits at the bottom left, a small Demo pill, and drops in Riverside College, Bendigo. Hit **Create the website** and it stays on this page. Websites already created appear as photo tiles, each opening that campaign's fundraiser list. The list is a row of tiles, each with **Manage**, and **Create a fundraiser** is the orange ribbon under them. Starting a fundraiser is a separate step from creating the website. No colour picker, no logo upload — every page looks like Kin.

## 2. Campaign — start the fundraiser

`/s/wattle-primary/admin`

This is the list of everything this campaign has running: a tile each for **Books for the library** and **New sports gear**, with what's raised so far and a **Manage** tap on each — several fundraisers can be open at once. Open **Manage** on Books for the library to see the finished campaign: the written letter, the amount raised, the row of books filling in, and the three amounts ($10, $25, $50) each with a **Change** tap. Point out:

- **Link for the promoter** — the address of this fundraiser, with **Copy link**. The owner gives that to a promoter. **Open** is only so you can step into their screen during the demo.
- **Hide dollar amounts on the leaderboard** — a switch, not a setting to dig for.
- The **thank-you leaderboard sits right there in the preview**, next to the campaign's public page — this screen is the only place anyone sees it now. **Show the wall** still fills the screen edge to edge with the top gift playing in large, for projecting. Esc or **Close** returns.

**Create a fundraiser** opens the one-question setup. Try "new basketball hoop" or "winter jackets" and watch the title, letter, goal, and three amounts write themselves. Under the gift amounts, point at **Donor game**: none (direct only), or one from the small catalog (stars, hearts, balloons). Publish lands straight on that new fundraiser's Manage screen.

## 3. Promoter — send the note

Open it from the copied promoter link, or from **Open** on the Manage screen. This is the promoter's screen, not the family's. Change **Your first name** and **Who you're sending it to** and watch the message update live. **Copy the message** puts one short note on the clipboard — the same words work in WhatsApp, a text, or Facebook — and that note contains the family's give link. **Print the note** opens a clean, large-type page for someone with no smartphone. Underneath: the best-time-to-send and today's-advice cards, both labelled as previews.

## 4. Family — give

Reached by tapping **Give to this fundraiser** on the public page (`/s/wattle-primary`), or the link a promoter sent. On the website that ribbon crosses the bottom of the photo. The letter sits in the white band under the photo. Other fundraisers are photo tiles under the heading "Also raising right now." A dark band quotes the child's letter.

The one page an aunty or uncle has to use. The bar up top says only **Give**, with the line "You are giving to this fundraiser." The photo fills the top, with "Hi Aunty June" over it and June in orange. Under the photo: when this fundraiser has a game, two clear choices — **Give directly** (the three named amounts) or **Play and give** (the campaign’s chosen light game, a few taps at $5 each; **Skip** returns to the choice). Then one first name and an orange **Give** ribbon. There is no wall here and no running total by name — a donor sees only their own "Thank you, {name}," not who else gave or how much. **Give again** resets the form so you can repeat the moment. The leaderboard is for the campaign owner only, back on the Manage screen.

## If asked about the game

The owner picks the game when starting the fundraiser. Each game is the same bar: a few large taps, never a wall between tapping and giving. **Skip** always works. There is no harder, score-chasing game — that was deliberate. The catalog can grow later; the give page only loads the one game this fundraiser chose.

## If asked about AI

Say exactly where it is, because none of it is a chatbot: the one-question campaign writer, the share message, and the two preview cards (best time, today's advice) on the promoter page. All three are labelled as previews — nothing here is a live prediction.
